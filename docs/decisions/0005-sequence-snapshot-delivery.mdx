---
title: 0005 Sequence snapshot delivery
description: Why live subscriptions are fed by sequence-numbered committed batches instead of filesystem notifications, and how the scan/live boundary stays exact.
---

## Context

strfry's ReqMonitor learns about new events by watching the database file with inotify. That lets several processes share one database, including imports, sync, and zero-downtime restarts. The cost is that ephemeral events must be stored briefly, and latency includes a filesystem notification round trip.

## Decision

- The writer is the sequencer. After each commit, it broadcasts the committed batch, events tagged with their seq, to the monitor shards in-process.
- The subscription's monitor shard assigns the snapshot S: the highest seq it has received. Registration and committed batches share the shard's ordered inbox, so a batch committed concurrently with registration can never fall between the scan and the live stream. Taking S from the store instead would open exactly that gap.
- A historical scan skips any key with seq above S, even across time slices that use different read transactions. The live stream delivers only seq above S, buffering until the scan's `EOSE`. Each event is delivered exactly once.
- Ephemeral events never touch storage. They are broadcast directly and have no seq, because no scan can return them.
- Multi-process cases are handled by tailing the sequence index. During a graceful drain (layer 4), the old process follows events with seq above its last seen value and feeds its remaining subscribers.

## Alternatives

- **inotify, as in strfry.** Rejected as the primary path: it adds latency and requires storing ephemeral events. Tailing by sequence keeps its one real benefit, multi-process visibility, where it is needed.
- **Broadcast without snapshots.** Rejected: time-sliced scans span several transactions, so events committed between slices would be delivered twice or missed.

## Consequences

- Live delivery latency is one channel hop after commit.
- The seq counter is persistent and never reused. Reusing it after a deletion would reopen the boundary race.
